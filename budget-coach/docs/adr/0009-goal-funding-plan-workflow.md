# A forward-looking Goal Funding Plan as a second workflow, routed by intent

The app had one workflow — the retrospective Monthly Review, which looks *back* at trailing spend and proposes Category Limits at ~110% of it. We added a second, **forward-looking** workflow, the **Goal Funding Plan**: given a Target the user states in chat ("save $2,000 by December", "can I afford a $1,200 laptop"), it checks whether that Target is feasible under the User's committed budget and, when cuts are needed, proposes re-fitted Category Limits for approval. The point of a second workflow was to make "which workflow runs?" a real decision — so the Coach now routes between the two by intent (LLM tool-selection), the genuinely new capability this exercises; everything downstream deliberately reuses existing patterns.

Savings-goal planning ("help me hit my goal") and affordability ("can I afford X") were unified into **one** workflow, because stripped of framing they are the same shape: *given a Target `{ amount, deadline?, kind }`, is it feasible, and which Category Limits must shrink to fund it?* Feasibility is measured against **Capacity** — `Declared Income − sum(Category Limits)`, the User's current committed free cash — deliberately **not** trailing spend. Monthly Review owns "past spend → limits"; the Funding Plan owns "committed limits → Target", so the two workflows compute on different bases and stay conceptually distinct rather than differing only in direction. When `Capacity ≥ required/month` the Target already fits and no cuts are proposed; otherwise the shortfall drives a tighter cap (`Declared Income − required`) and Category Limits are scaled down proportionally to fit it — **the exact ADR-0007 mechanism**, just driven by a Target instead of a manually-set Savings Goal, so both workflows share one scaling primitive (`proposeCategoryLimits`).

On approval the re-fitted Category Limits always persist. A **savings** Target additionally overwrites the Savings Goal with its `required/month` figure (a dated savings goal collapses into the recurring per-Period Savings Goal the domain already has — see the refined glossary entry); a **purchase** Target is one-off and persists only the limits, leaving the Savings Goal untouched.

## Related decision: narrowing the financial-advice guardrail's `fund` keyword

The Funding Plan makes "fund my trip" / "help me fund my goal" natural User utterances, which collided with a pre-existing false-positive: `financialAdviceGuardrail` matched the bare substring `"fund"` as a financial-instrument keyword, so `emergency fund`, `fund my trip`, and even `refund` / `funding` tripped it whenever a decision word co-occurred. We narrowed the keyword to its investment-only forms — `"mutual fund"`, `"index fund"`, `"hedge fund"` — removing the ambiguous bare token. Genuine investment intent still trips on its *other* instrument keywords (`stock`, `crypto`, `shares`, …), and the output-side `regulatedAdviceOutputGuardrail` plus the `coachScopeScorer` LLM judge remain as backstops for advice-flavored *responses*.

## Considered Options

- **Two separate workflows (savings planner + affordability checker)** — rejected: identical shape once reduced to a Target; one workflow with a `kind` discriminator avoids duplicating the whole suspend/apply pipeline.
- **Trailing-spend Capacity basis (like Monthly Review)** — rejected: it would make the two workflows differ only in direction over the same numbers; the committed-limits basis gives them genuinely distinct jobs.
- **Discretionary-first cuts** (trim Dining/Entertainment/Shopping before Groceries/Utilities/Housing/Health) — rejected for now: introduces a new "which Categories are discretionary" policy the model doesn't have elsewhere; proportional scaling reuses ADR-0007 and treats Categories symmetrically like everything else does. A reasonable v2.
- **Best-effort plan when the Target is infeasible** (`required ≥ Declared Income`) — rejected: an approval card that can't actually reach the Target is misleading. The workflow bails before the approval gate with a plain message (extend the deadline or lower the Target), mirroring the existing "goal not achievable" guard in `approveBudgetTool`.
- **A router agent or deterministic router to pick the workflow** — rejected: LLM tool-selection is the honest meaning of "the agent determines which workflow", matches the Coach-as-front-door architecture, and adds no new infrastructure. Reliability comes from two sharply-worded tool descriptions plus one contrast paragraph in the Coach's base instructions, not a separate classifier.
- **Sharing the `approvalGate` / `applyOrDiscard` steps between both workflows** — rejected: the two suspend payloads and apply-logic already differ (the Funding Plan may also write the Savings Goal) and will drift further; premature sharing across two suspendable workflows is exactly the coupling that makes suspend/resume wiring hard to follow. `goalFundingWorkflow` keeps parallel copies.
- **A frontend input form to collect the Target** (like `provideSavingsGoal`) — rejected: the Target is almost always already stated in the triggering sentence, so the Coach extracts `{ amount, deadline?, kind }` into the `planFunding` tool call and only asks in chat if the amount is missing.

## Consequences

- A new `goalFundingWorkflow` (`resolveTarget → analyzeCapacity → proposeReallocation → approvalGate (suspends) → applyOrDiscard`), a new Coach tool `planFunding` that suspends server-side so `useInterrupt` renders the approval card, and a routing paragraph added to `BASE_INSTRUCTIONS` contrasting it with `approveBudget`.
- **`pendingApproval` is now shared by two workflows.** The single working-memory slot must record *which* workflow owns the pending run (or use distinct slots) so each tool's resume path targets the right workflow — otherwise `approveBudget` could try to resume a Funding Plan run, or vice versa. The invariant becomes "at most one approval Pending Approval across *both* workflows for a User at a time."
- `financialAdviceGuardrail` no longer blocks the bare word `fund`; a test asserts `refund` / `emergency fund` + a decision word do not block, guarding against a future re-broadening.
